# Our Trailer, Our Rules

> You and your friend act in the same movie—without knowing each other’s script.

## Concrete example

One person thinks they are filming a spy thriller. The other thinks they are filming a breakup drama. The combined trailer is titled **The Last Shared Password**.

## User flow

1. One person buys a proposed **$5.99 duo trailer** and sends an invitation. The second person joins free.
2. Each gets private, coordinated filming instructions without seeing the other person's prompts.
3. Person A: Look behind you and whisper, “They found us.”
4. Person B: Look offended and say, “You promised this wouldn’t happen again.”
5. Each records a few short clips through guided screens.
6. The app cuts the performances together: an alarmed look, an offended reply, dramatic music, and the movie title.
7. Both watch the surprise reveal and can download the trailer. Public sharing should require both participants' approval.

## Participation and AI roles

People perform the scenes and supply the comic delivery. The app coordinates prompts and edits actual performances. AI may assist with captions or personalisation; full AI video generation is not required. This is not a selfie-upload animation product.

## Distinctive point

Hidden, coordinated instructions cause the participants to set up each other's jokes without knowing it. The game before the video is the differentiator, not “AI video” or a polished edit alone.

## Smallest proposed version

Guided recording, a small set of coordinated secret prompts, real footage, reliable edit templates, music, title cards, and AI-assisted captions where useful. Skip full generated video. Test willingness to act and finish before sophisticated editing.

## Competitive evidence

[CapCut: Couple Memories templates](https://www.capcut.com/explore/couple-memories) offers templates for turning photos and clips into couple-memory montages, with custom text and music. This establishes competition for the finished output, not evidence of the exact hidden-instruction multiplayer game.

## Risks — interpretation

- **Existing competition: High for the output.** Polished personal videos are already easy to produce; editing alone is not distinctive.
- **Sherlocking: High.** A video or social platform could add invitations, secret recording prompts, and a combined reveal.
- **Participation: Very high.** Two people need to feel comfortable acting on camera and complete several recordings.
- **Quality:** Lighting, audio, timing, and awkward delivery may disappoint buyers even if the edit functions correctly.
- **Privacy:** Obtain both participants' approval before public sharing and clearly disclose recording handling.
- **Defensibility:** Coordinated gameplay differentiates the experience, but does not yet provide a strong moat.

## Recommendation and validation

The most distinctive creative experience of the five, but the hardest to get people to finish. One of the two preferred experiments. Test whether a buyer can recruit a partner and complete the recordings before investing in fancy editing. A concrete shareable output is promising; demand is still unvalidated.

## Status and commercial assumptions

This is a proposed experience, not a validated market opportunity. Prices are test proposals, not proven willingness to pay. Risk ratings are qualitative judgments, not measured probabilities. Competitor facts below come from the linked sources checked during the discussion; exact feature parity, revenue, and market coverage have not been comprehensively verified.

The intended model is a playful, participatory, one-off purchase rather than a subscription or a passive upload-and-wait AI generator. One buyer covers invited participants; invitations should not require another payment. Lead with the experience, but clearly disclose AI involvement, price, deliverable, recording handling, and failure/refund terms before checkout. Do not promise policies before implementing them.

Sherlocking means an established platform or adjacent product incorporates the central feature, reducing the reason to buy this standalone product. A distinctive experience is not automatically a durable moat.
